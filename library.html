Writing / Sample01 — Read

The first word is the hardest

On beginning before the sentence is ready, and letting a thought become real by writing it down.

 Open the piece ↗

There is a particular silence before you begin. It is not empty. It is full of the sentences you have rehearsed, revised, and quietly decided not to say.

The trick is not to wait until the silence goes away. Start while it is still there. Write one honest line. Say one unfinished thought. A beginning does not need to be impressive; it only needs to be yours.

After that, the next word has somewhere to stand. Then the next. Before long, the thing you were trying to find is no longer hidden in your head. It is here, where someone else can meet it.

Field note / Sample02 — Read

A morning, measured in small things

A short reflection on showing up, keeping pace, and noticing the person beside you.

 Open the piece ↗

Some mornings begin before the day has decided what it will be. The air is still cool; the streets have not yet filled in. You arrive with your own reasons for being there.

A shared run does not ask everyone to move at the same speed. It asks us to start together, leave space for different paces, and notice when someone could use a little company.

The distance matters. So does the photograph afterward, the first deep breath, the ordinary proof that we kept a small promise to ourselves and to one another.

Conversation / Sample03 — Read

Leave room for the second answer

A question can open a room. Listening carefully is how we make space for the reply.

 Open the piece ↗

In a good conversation, the first answer is not always the whole answer. A person may need a moment to move past what sounds certain and find the part they really mean.

That is why listening is an action. It means not rushing to fill every pause, not preparing your response while someone else is still speaking, and being curious enough to ask what sits underneath.

We will not agree about everything. We can still make a room where disagreement is thoughtful, questions are welcome, and people leave with more to consider than they brought in.

Writing / Sample04 — Read

Practice, not permission

You do not need to become someone else to speak with confidence. You need a place to practice.

 Open the piece ↗

Confidence is often described as if it arrives all at once: a switch flipped, a fear removed, a perfectly steady voice. Most of the time, it looks smaller than that.

It looks like volunteering for one sentence. Reading a paragraph aloud even when your hands shake. Saying, “I am not sure yet,” and staying in the conversation long enough to get sure.

Practice does not make every moment easy. It makes the next moment familiar. And familiar is sometimes enough to help us take the step we were waiting for.
